Plaintiff Jerry Nelson (“Plaintiff”), appearing pro se, respectfully submits this Objection to Defendants’ Motion to Dismiss. Because Plaintiff’s Complaint states multiple actionable federal claims and any deficiencies are curable through amendment, the Motion should be denied. In the alternative, Plaintiff requests leave to amend under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 15(a)(2).

# **I. INTRODUCTION**

Plaintiff seeks redress for the unconstitutional and discriminatory imposition and collection of excessive fines by Defendants, a County government and its agents. The fines were grossly disproportionate to the alleged violations, imposed without proper notice or hearing, and administered through an unlawful administrative process that denied Plaintiff due process and reasonable accommodations for his disabilities.

Plaintiff’s primary remedy sought is **return of the unlawfully collected fines**, plus interest. Federal law expressly authorizes this Court to order repayment under **42 U.S.C. § 1983**, **Title II of the ADA**, and **Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act**.

# **II. LEGAL STANDARD**

A complaint survives a motion to dismiss when it states a claim that is plausible on its face. *Ashcroft v. Iqbal*, 556 U.S. 662 (2009). Pro se pleadings must be liberally construed and held to less stringent standards. *Erickson v. Pardus*, 551 U.S. 89 (2007). Dismissal with prejudice is improper unless amendment would be futile.

# **III. PLAINTIFF CAN AMEND TO CURE ANY DEFICIENCIES**

Any alleged defects in Plaintiff’s Complaint are readily curable. Plaintiff now has access to legal‑research assistance and can amend to plead the correct causes of action, including:

- 42 U.S.C. § 1983 – Excessive Fines (Eighth Amendment)

- 42 U.S.C. § 1983 – Procedural and Substantive Due Process (Fourteenth Amendment)

- 42 U.S.C. § 1983 – Unconstitutional Taking (Fifth Amendment)

- Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act

- Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act

- Supplemental Oregon state‑law claims

Because amendment would not be futile, dismissal with prejudice is unwarranted.

# **IV. PLAUSIBLE CLAIM UNDER THE EIGHTH AMENDMENT (EXCESSIVE FINES)**

Plaintiff alleges that Defendants imposed fines nearly equivalent to the cost of a home in 2008 and far exceeding the cost of any remediation. Such disproportionate penalties state a plausible claim under the **Excessive Fines Clause**, which applies to counties through the Fourteenth Amendment.

Under § 1983, Plaintiff is entitled to **compensatory damages and restitution** of all unlawfully collected fines.

# **V. PLAUSIBLE DUE PROCESS CLAIMS (FOURTEENTH AMENDMENT)**

Defendants mailed notices to the wrong address, denied Plaintiff reasonable notice and opportunity to be heard, and operated an administrative system in which they acted as investigator, prosecutor, adjudicator, and collector. Plaintiff was never afforded a meaningful hearing or ADA accommodations.

These allegations state claims for:

- **Procedural Due Process** – deprivation of property without notice or hearing

- **Substantive Due Process** – arbitrary and punitive enforcement

Lane County Government doesn’t have an Administrative Procedures Act, the State of Oregon does, but the Procedural and Substantive Due Process Rights protected by the APA still apply in this context.

Federal courts routinely order **return of money collected without due process**.

# **VI. PLAUSIBLE TAKINGS CLAIM (FIFTH AMENDMENT)**

Defendants seized Plaintiff’s money through fines imposed without lawful authority or constitutional process. This constitutes an uncompensated taking for public use. Plaintiff seeks **just compensation**, including repayment of all money taken and interest.

# **VII. PLAUSIBLE ADA AND REHABILITATION ACT CLAIMS**

Plaintiff is a qualified individual with disabilities. Defendants, a public entity receiving federal funds, discriminated against Plaintiff by:

- Imposing escalating fines because Plaintiff’s disabilities limited his ability to maintain the property

- Failing to provide reasonable accommodations

- Denying meaningful access to code‑enforcement processes

- Using punitive enforcement instead of accommodation

Title II of the ADA and Section 504 authorize **monetary damages** and **restitution** against counties.

# **VIII. STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS DOES NOT BAR THE CLAIMS**

Defendants’ limitations argument fails for multiple reasons:

1.  **Continuing Violation Doctrine** – each fine is a new actionable violation.

2.  **Discovery Rule** – Plaintiff did not receive notice because Defendants mailed notices to the wrong address.

3.  **Equitable Tolling** – Defendants’ actions prevented Plaintiff from discovering the injury.

These are fact‑intensive issues inappropriate for dismissal.

# **IX. PLAINTIFF IS NOT REQUIRED TO EXHAUST ADMINISTRATIVE REMEDIES**

Exhaustion is not required for:

- § 1983 constitutional claims

- ADA Title II claims

- Rehabilitation Act claims

The Administrative Procedure Act does not apply to county code enforcement.

# **X. MONELL LIABILITY IS ADEQUATELY ALLEGED**

Defendants’ actions were taken pursuant to County policies, practices, or customs, including:

1.  Imposing excessive fines for minor violations

2.  Mailing notices to incorrect addresses

3.  Denying meaningful hearings

4.  Combining investigative, prosecutorial, and adjudicative functions

5.  Failing to train staff on ADA compliance

6.  Enforcing code violations without reasonable accommodations

These policies were the moving force behind Plaintiff’s injuries.

# **XI. REPAYMENT OF FINES IS A PROPER FEDERAL REMEDY**

Federal courts may order repayment of unlawfully collected fines as:

- **Compensatory damages under § 1983**

- **Just compensation under the Takings Clause**

- **Monetary damages under the ADA and Rehabilitation Act**

- **Restitution under the Declaratory Judgment Act**

Because Defendants collected money through unconstitutional and discriminatory practices, repayment is the appropriate remedy.

# **XII. CONCLUSION**

For the foregoing reasons, Plaintiff respectfully requests that the Court:

1.  **Deny** Defendants’ Motion to Dismiss; or

2.  In the alternative, **grant leave to amend** the Complaint.

Plaintiff stands ready to provide medical documentation, financial records, and other evidence during discovery.
