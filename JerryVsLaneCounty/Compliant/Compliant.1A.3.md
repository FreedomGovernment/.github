# FIRST AMENDED COMPLAINT

This action seeks recovery of civil penalties collected through Lane County code enforcement, relief concerning additional challenged penalties and liens, and remedies for denial of meaningful access to enforcement and appeal procedures because of disability.

UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT
DISTRICT OF OREGON
EUGENE DIVISION

JERRY NELSON,
Plaintiff,

v.

LANE COUNTY GOVERNMENT,
Defendant.

Case No. 6:25-cv-02031-MTK

FIRST AMENDED COMPLAINT
Jury Trial Demanded

## Parties, Jurisdiction, and Venue

1. I, Jerry Nelson, am an individual residing in Lane County, Oregon.

2. Defendant Lane County Government ("County") is an Oregon county and a public entity responsible for the County code-enforcement, civil-penalty, administrative-appeal, and lien practices challenged here.

3. This Court has jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. §§ 1331 and 1343 over my claims under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 and Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act ("ADA"), 42 U.S.C. §§ 12131–12134.

4. Under 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b)(2), venue is proper because "a substantial part of the events or omissions giving rise to the claim occurred, and the subject property is situated, in Lane County within the District of Oregon." Venue arises from the acts/property location rather than the constitutional clauses themselves.

## Facts

### County Enforcement and Notice

5. I owned property identified in my proposed amended complaint as 82602 Howe Lane, Creswell, Oregon 97426, subject to Lane County Land Management Division File No. 509-CA22-00334.

6. The County alleged violations under Lane Code § 9.584 (renumbered LC § 9.057.584) concerning the alleged accumulation, collection, storage, or disposal of solid waste or other debris.

7. On or about April 6, 2023, the County issued an Order to Comply requiring removal of identified waste and debris, with a stated compliance date of April 10, 2023. It addressed the order to 410 W. 27th Avenue, Eugene, Oregon 97405, where I had not lived for approximately seventeen years.

8. During the relevant period, my mailing address was 2725 City View Street, Eugene, Oregon 97405. I assert that material enforcement notices were sent to my former address rather than my current mailing address.

9. On or about April 27, 2023, pursuant to Lane Code § 5.017 and Lane Manual § 5.020, the County issued a Notice of Failure to Comply stating that a civil penalty of approximately $1,680 per day would begin on May 3, 2023, and continue to accumulate until certified compliance.

10. The County alleges that I signed a certified-mail return receipt dated June 2, 2023. I don't remember signing that receipt or opening the letter. I do not assert that lack of recollection establishes that the signature is false.

11. The Notice of Failure to Comply described a right to appeal. I did not appeal File No. 509-CA22-00334. My September 2025 appeal concerned a different matter, File No. 509-CA25-00339, involving 88130 Green Hill Road.

12. I assert that my mental-health limitations and difficulty using computers and completing appeal paperwork impaired my ability to understand and use the notice, compliance, and appeal processes. Concurrent City and County enforcement demands overwhelmed me. This complaint challenges the County's conduct, not unidentified City conduct.

13. I assert that the County did not make sufficiently clear what I needed to do to comply and began imposing accumulating penalties without reasonably effective advance notice and a meaningful opportunity for me to contest the asserted violations. My challenge concerns the adequacy and accessibility of the process actually available, not an assertion that the notice contained no appeal language.

### Penalties, Collection, and Cleanup

14. On or about September 21, 2023, the County signed an order concerning accumulated penalties. On September 22, 2023, it recorded a lien.

15. On or about November 1, 2023, when I sold property at 1625 Bailey Hill Road, the County collected approximately $118,759, including interest, from the sale proceeds in connection with File No. 509-CA22-00334.

16. I estimate that correcting the conditions at the subject property would have cost approximately $10,000 for labor, equipment, and disposal fees.

17. I undertook cleanup by renting dumpsters and loading equipment, using a front-end loader and my truck, and paying workers. I have receipts concerning those efforts. Trespassers and squatters contributed to the accumulation of materials at the property.

18. I assert that the accumulated penalties were grossly disproportionate to the gravity of the noncriminal property-code violation, considering the estimated remediation cost, my cleanup efforts, and the contribution of third parties. I challenge the punitive penalty component, rather than assuming every charge is a punitive fine.

19. I identify a separate instrument dated May 23, 2025, recorded as Reception No. 2025-015117, stating an amount of approximately $134,400. I assert that additional penalties continued after the November 2023 collection. I do not have a complete itemization of all subsequent penalties and liens.

20. The County's later state foreclosure papers in case 25CV55934 identify property at 8620 Howe Lane, Creswell, Oregon, and a May 23, 2025 nuisance lien. The photographed corrected-judgment papers state a money award of $139,172.12. Those stated addresses and amounts are not treated here as interchangeable with the address in paragraph 5 or the sum collected in paragraph 15.

21. I seek recovery for the County's underlying enforcement and collection conduct. I do not ask this Court to act as an appellate court reviewing the state foreclosure judgment. My request for relief against continuing penalties and liens concerns the County conduct challenged in this action, subject to this Court's authority to grant that relief.

### Disability, Requests for Modification, and County Knowledge

22. I have diagnosed PTSD, hoarding disorder, schizotypal personality disorder, and depression. These conditions substantially limit concentrating, organizing, thinking, and completing multistep tasks. They affected my ability to manage cleanup and use complex enforcement and appeal procedures.

23. On April 15, 2025, I personally delivered a handwritten letter to the County describing my mental-health limitations and requesting suspension of penalties so I could devote my resources to cleanup, hire assistance, and document progress. County personnel made a photocopy; I retained the original letter.

24. I also attended two meetings in a County conference room with code-enforcement personnel and a hearings official. I showed medical records and a medication list and requested more time to comply and the opportunity to devote money otherwise demanded as fines to removal of the materials. I do not presently recall the meeting dates, participants' names, or their exact responses.

25. I assert that County personnel verbally acknowledged awareness of my condition but did not provide the requested modifications. I do not claim to recall specific words of denial. I assert that the County did not respond to my April 15, 2025 letter and that additional penalties continued afterward.

26. I contend that additional cleanup time, suspension of additional penalties while documented cleanup progressed, notices at my current address, and communication suited to my limitations would have allowed me meaningful access to compliance and hearing procedures while permitting lawful enforcement to continue.

27. The November 2023 collection preceded the April 15, 2025 letter. I don't attribute that earlier collection to failure to respond to the later letter. My disability-access claim concerns enforcement after the County learned of my disability and need for modification; the undated meetings do not establish an earlier knowledge date.

### County Practices and Property Rights

28. The County assessed daily penalties, recorded liens, and collected the sale proceeds pursuant to its codified enforcement framework set forth in Lane Code Chapter 5 (including LC §§ 5.010, 5.017, and 5.020) and Lane Manual Chapter 5 (including LM §§ 5.015 and 5.020), as well as the September 21, 2023 enforcement order. I challenge the County's implementation of that penalty system and the resulting official assessments and collections, not merely an employee's conduct by reason of employment.

29. The County states that its service practice was to mail enforcement documents to the property owner's address shown in current tax records. I challenge that practice as applied to my notices at the former address and its connection to deprivation of my money and property interests without adequate notice and opportunity to contest the assessment.

30. I assert that the County's enforcement process combined investigation, prosecution, administrative determination, and collection while failing to afford me a meaningful and accessible opportunity to contest the charges. I dispute the sufficiency of photographic accusations as a basis for the penalties and contend that the County must substantiate its charges before depriving me of property. I do not assert that combining administrative functions, by itself, proves unconstitutional bias.

## Count I — Title II of the ADA: Denial of Reasonable Modification and Meaningful Access

31. I incorporate paragraphs 1–30.

32. I'm a qualified individual with disabilities for purposes of access to the County's code-enforcement, compliance, administrative-appeal, and hearing processes. The County is a public entity whose services, programs, and activities are subject to 42 U.S.C. § 12132.

33. Because of the limitations described in paragraph 22, I needed reasonable modifications to have meaningful access to those processes. I request the modifications described in paragraphs 23–26. The County had notice of the disability-related need no later than delivery of the April 15, 2025 letter.

34. I assert that the County failed to respond to that letter or provide the requested modifications while continuing penalties, thereby denying meaningful access by reason of disability. I contend that the requested modifications would not have required abandonment of lawful cleanup requirements.

35. I contend that the County's failure to address my disclosed limitations and request while continuing enforcement constituted deliberate indifference to my right of meaningful access. I seek relief for resulting disability-related injury after the County had notice, including additional pecuniary loss established at trial, without attributing the November 2023 payment to the April 2025 letter.

## Count II — Fourteenth Amendment Procedural Due Process Under 42 U.S.C. § 1983

36. I incorporate paragraphs 1–30.

37. I had protected property interests in my sale proceeds and real property. Acting under color of state law, the County assessed accumulating penalties, recorded a lien, and collected approximately $118,759, including interest, from my November 2023 sale proceeds.

38. I assert that the County's notice and opportunity to contest those deprivations were constitutionally inadequate as applied, for the reasons in paragraphs 7–13 and 28–30. I seek adjudication of the actual service and accessibility of the process, including the County's asserted June 2, 2023 receipt and the appeal opportunity stated in the notice.

39. I assert that the challenged County enforcement and notice practices caused the assessment and collection without adequate process, depriving me of approximately $118,759 and burdening my property with additional challenged penalties and liens.

## Count III — Eighth Amendment Excessive Fines Under 42 U.S.C. § 1983

40. I incorporate paragraphs 1–30.

41. I assert that the accumulating civil penalties served at least partly to punish or deter noncompliance and therefore constituted fines subject to the Eighth Amendment, applicable to the County through the Fourteenth Amendment.

42. I assert that the punitive penalties were grossly disproportionate to the gravity of the underlying violation for the reasons in paragraphs 16–18. The estimated remediation cost is one consideration, not my sole asserted measure of constitutional excessiveness.

43. I assert that the County imposed and collected those penalties through its official penalty system and enforcement order. I seek recovery of the unconstitutional portion of the approximately $118,759 collected, appropriate interest, and relief concerning additional excessive penalties and related liens without duplicate recovery.

## Count IV — Fourteenth Amendment Substantive Due Process Under 42 U.S.C. § 1983

44. I incorporate paragraphs 1–30. Separately from my notice and Excessive Fines Clause objections, I contend that the County's enforcement was arbitrary in demanding escalating sums while obscuring the steps necessary to comply and failing to address disclosed disability-related barriers and documented cleanup efforts. I seek protection against arbitrary deprivation of my property interests, not an exemption from lawful property regulation or a claim that the County must reimburse every cleanup expense.

45. I assert that the challenged official enforcement practices caused the property and monetary injuries described above. I plead this contention in the alternative, to the extent it addresses arbitrary conduct not governed exclusively by a more specific constitutional protection, without duplicating recovery under my other claims.

## Requested Relief

46. I request judgment restoring the unconstitutional penalties collected from my November 2023 sale proceeds, approximately $118,759 including interest, to the extent proved and legally recoverable, without counting interest or losses twice.

47. I request a declaration concerning the challenged County practices and appropriate relief against additional unlawful penalties and related liens, including release or correction of liens to the extent authorized in this action. I do not seek blanket removal of unrelated lawful liens.

48. I request compensatory damages for additional proven injury attributable to Defendant's conduct, including the additional damages demanded in my proposed pleading in the amount of $1,000,000 to the extent supported by proof and available under the applicable claims. I request no duplicate award and no punitive damages against the County.

49. I request prejudgment and postjudgment interest as allowed by law, costs, attorney fees if legally recoverable, and other relief the Court is authorized to grant.

50. I demand a Jury trial on all issues so triable.
