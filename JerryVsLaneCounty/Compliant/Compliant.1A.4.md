## Parties, Jurisdiction, and Venue

1. I, Jerry Nelson, am an individual residing in Lane County, Oregon.

2. Defendant Lane County Government ("County") is an Oregon county and a public entity responsible for the County code-enforcement, civil-penalty, administrative-appeal, and lien practices challenged in this action.

3. This Court has jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. §§ 1331 and 1343 over my claims under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 and Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act ("ADA"), 42 U.S.C. §§ 12131–12134.

4. Venue is proper in the District of Oregon under 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b)(2) because a substantial part of the events and omissions giving rise to my claims occurred in Lane County, within this District. The challenged enforcement and affected properties are located in Lane County. Divisional venue lies in the Eugene Division.

## Facts — Separate County Enforcement Matters

5. My Howe Lane property is located at 82602 Howe Lane, Creswell, Oregon 97426. It was the subject of Lane County Land Management Division File No. 509-CA22-00334.

6. The County imposed two large fines on me at the same time concerning two separate, noncontiguous properties. The concurrent demands burdened my ability to organize, finance, and carry out cleanup and to understand and respond to the separate enforcement matters. The property associated with the second concurrent fine is 88130 Greenhill Road Eugene OR 97402. I'm not sure exactly how long the period was.

I challenge the County’s notice, access procedures, punitive assessments, and collection practices as applied to me. Each challenged assessment must be connected to the property, charged condition, and assessment period to which it actually relates.

## Count II — Fourteenth Amendment Procedural Due Process Under 42 U.S.C. § 1983

48. I incorporate paragraphs 1–42.

49. I had protected property interests in my sale proceeds and real property. Acting under color of state law, the County assessed daily penalties, recorded a lien, and collected approximately $118,759, including interest, from my November 2023 sale proceeds.

50. I assert that the notice and opportunity to contest these deprivations were constitutionally inadequate as applied. My challenge includes the former-address mailings, the asserted commencement of penalties before the June 2 receipt, the accessibility and timing of the appeal process, and the denied opportunity to contest the factual basis and amount of the assessment. A description of an appeal on paper does not resolve whether I had reasonably effective notice and a meaningful opportunity to use it.

51. I assert that the County’s official notice practice and its implementation of the assessment and collection procedures caused the deprivation without adequate process. I seek recovery for the monetary and property injury caused by those practices, including penalties I could have contested and addressed through effective notice and accessible procedures, without presuming that every procedural defect necessarily entitles me to repayment of every charge.

## Count III — Eighth Amendment Excessive Fines Under 42 U.S.C. § 1983

52. I incorporate paragraphs 1–42.

53. 56. I assert that the County imposed and collected the challenged penalties through its official penalty framework and assessment orders. ## Count IV — Fourteenth Amendment Substantive Due Process Under 42 U.S.C. § 1983

57. I incorporate paragraphs 1–42. Independently of my notice and Excessive Fines Clause objections, I contend that the County arbitrarily used its enforcement authority to demand escalating sums while failing to make compliance requirements sufficiently clear or address my disclosed disability-related barriers and documented cleanup efforts.

58. I contend that the challenged conduct lacked a reasonable justification tied to legitimate nuisance abatement and the State has strict scrutany to prove it is harmful or offensive because it is my property protected by Fifth and Fourteenth Amendment Due Process. Substantial refers to size, quantity, or extent, describing something large, solid, or significant in amount. Substantive refers to meaning, essence, or content, describing something real, meaningful, or fundamental in nature.  I do not contend that ownership exempts me from lawful property regulation, only the heightened evidence burden protecting my property.

59. I plead this claim in the alternative only insofar as the arbitrary conduct is not governed exclusively by a more specific constitutional protection. I preserve the claim without treating every notice defect, excessive fine, or failure to modify as an independent substantive-due-process violation, and without duplicating recovery.
