## [SCENE 2: THE LEGAL ARCHITECTURE]
**[CAMERA: Cale in the workshop/lab. He’s holding a 3D printed part or a CNC tool. Relaxed but authoritative.]**

**CALE:**
I’m an engineer first. And in engineering, you don't just look at the result; you look at the logic. 

**[GRAPHIC: "Preponderance of Evidence" (50.1%) vs. "Substantial Evidence Burden"]**

**CALE:**
The State wants to use "Preponderance of Evidence." That’s the "more likely than not" rule. In a legal sense, that’s like having a thermostat with no sensor. If the temperature crosses a threshold, the machine just kicks on. It doesn't care about the reality of the room; it just follows a binary switch.

**[TERMINAL CAPTURE: Scrolling through the "Petition for Panel Rehearing" document.]**

**CALE (V.O.):**
When the State uses preponderance, it allows for "pseudo-judicial" proceedings. It allows them to strip your rights based on opinions, tribalism, and sloppy fact-finding. We are fighting for a **Substantial Evidence Burden**. We need the "sensor" back in the thermostat. We need the truth to be a requirement, not an option.
